Principles

Design principles

The north stars that adjudicate every visual decision in Cadence. When two approaches both "work," these break the tie. They're the reason the system looks the way it does — a mission-control console, not a marketing site.

1

Information density over decoration

Every pixel earns its place. Operators read this screen to fly spacecraft — pack in signal, cut ornament. Whitespace is for hierarchy, not for breathing room.

2

Legible under stress

People scan this during anomalies. Maintain the contrast tiers (informational text at /70 minimum), keep hierarchy obvious, and never make someone hunt for the number that matters.

3

Status is always a color

The four status colors — critical, warning, success, info — mean the same thing everywhere: badges, borders, dots, charts. A color is never decorative; if it's a status color, it's reporting state.

4

Sharp and technical

2px corners, uppercase tracked labels, monospace for every data value. The HUD aesthetic is the brand — lean into it rather than softening it toward a generic dashboard.

5

Glow means "live"

Glow is reserved for navigation targets and live / alert signals. It signals "this is active or actionable." Buttons never glow. Decorative glow erodes the signal everywhere else.

6

One way to do each thing

A closed component set and frozen CSS. Compose new looks from what exists — daisyUI classes, HUD utilities, the component layer — rather than inventing selectors. Consistency beats cleverness, and the system stays enforceable.
