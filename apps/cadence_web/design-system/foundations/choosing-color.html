Choosing color

You don't pick colors for a console — you allocate them to jobs, and most choices are constrained, not free. These are the selection rules, and the resolution of the system's biggest open color question: cyan's double duty.

The selection rules

  • 1Allocate by job, then count. Decide the roles (brand · status · neutral · data-viz); the roles tell you how many colors — not the other way around.
  • 2Color is a scarce signal — spend it like one. A dense console is ~90% neutral so color means something. If everything's colored, nothing reads as urgent. (This is the rule cyan broke.)
  • 3Status hues are dictated, not chosen. red=danger · yellow=caution · green=nominal · blue=info are learned control-system conventions. Only the shade is yours.
  • 4Never rely on hue alone. ~8% of men have red-green CVD — and that's your status pair. Separate red & green by lightness (you do: red 62% / green 76%), and always pair color with shape or label.
  • 5Cool = ambient, warm = exception. The screen stays calm cyan/ink until something's wrong, then warm red/yellow grabs the eye. Don't break this.
  • 6Brand is the only free zone — and still constrained. A brand color must never collide with status meaning, and must stay distinct from status hues. (Watch: action-cyan 210 vs info-blue 250 are close — keep them apart.)

Decision — resolving cyan's double duty

Saturation carries the interactive signal.

Cyan was doing two jobs — brand atmosphere (borders, labels, grid, values) and the interactive signal (links, buttons, focus). When cyan is everywhere, "is it cyan?" can't mean "can I act?" The fix isn't to remove cyan — it's to make saturated cyan = act / live, and demote structural chrome to low-chroma (a cyan-tinted neutral). The signal moves from hue → saturation.

△ Rule: interactive chroma ≥ 0.10; structural chrome chroma ≤ 0.03. The test: could a user mistake this for something clickable? If yes, it's too saturated.

The honest tradeoff

This is an aesthetic shift: the "cyan glow everywhere" maximalism becomes restrained, with cyan as punctuation. You're trading some sci-fi density for a working signal — which is the right trade under your own principles (density over decoration, legible under stress). The identity survives in the Tokyo-night base, the sharp 2px, the monospace data, the pink accent, and cyan marking exactly what's alive. It's a 🎯 target — the reallocation lands when the code is reconciled to this.
